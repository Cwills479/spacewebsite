Overview

The Sun is a massive, glowing sphere of hot plasma at the center of our solar system, providing the energy that sustains life on Earth. Composed primarily of hydrogen and helium, it generates light and heat through nuclear fusion, converting hydrogen into helium in its core. This process releases an immense amount of energy, radiating outward as sunlight, which drives weather patterns, supports photosynthesis in plants, and influences the climate. Despite being an average-sized star, the Sun's gravitational pull keeps the planets, asteroids, and comets in orbit, maintaining the structure of the solar system. Its surface, known as the photosphere, emits the light we see, while dynamic features like sunspots, solar flares, and coronal mass ejections reveal the Sun’s intense magnetic activity.

Key Facts:

  • Distance from the Sun: 57.9 million km
  • Diameter: 4,879 km
  • Orbital period: 88 Earth days
  • Surface temperature: -173°C to 427°C
