Overview

Neptune, the eighth and farthest planet from the Sun, is a deep blue ice giant known for its fierce winds and dynamic atmosphere. Its striking blue color comes from the methane in its atmosphere, which absorbs red light and reflects blue. Despite being about 30 times farther from the Sun than Earth, Neptune is an active planet with supersonic winds reaching speeds of over 1,200 miles per hour (2,000 kilometers per hour), the fastest in the solar system. The planet's atmosphere features large storms, including the famous Great Dark Spot, similar to Jupiter's Great Red Spot, though these storms appear and disappear over time. Neptune's core is surrounded by layers of water, ammonia, and methane ices, contributing to its classification as an ice giant. It has 14 known moons, the largest being Triton, which orbits in the opposite direction of Neptune's rotation and is believed to be a captured object from the Kuiper Belt. Despite its great distance, Neptune's gravitational influence helps shape the orbits of objects in the outer solar system, and it continues to fascinate astronomers with its mysterious, stormy nature.

Key Facts:

  • Distance from the Sun: 4.4713 Billion km
  • Diameter: 49,244 km
  • Orbital period: 165 Earth Years
  • Surface temperature: -201°C
