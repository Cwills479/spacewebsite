Overview

Saturn, the sixth planet from the Sun, is best known for its stunning and complex ring system, which makes it one of the most visually striking planets in the solar system. Like Jupiter, Saturn is a gas giant composed mainly of hydrogen and helium, with no solid surface beneath its thick atmosphere of swirling clouds and storms. Its rings, made of countless ice and rock particles, stretch out for hundreds of thousands of miles but are surprisingly thin, sometimes just a few meters thick. Saturn is less dense than water, meaning it would float if there were a large enough body of water to hold it. The planet has a strong magnetic field and emits more energy than it receives from the Sun due to internal processes, like the gradual compression of its core. Saturn is also home to at least 146 moons, including Titan, the second-largest moon in the solar system, which has a dense atmosphere and liquid methane lakes, and Enceladus, which spews icy plumes into space, hinting at a subsurface ocean that could harbor life.

Key Facts:

  • Distance from the Sun: 1.438 Billion km
  • Diameter: 74,897 km
  • Orbital period: 29.4 Earth Years
  • Surface temperature: -178°C
